How it works

Time
The bar along the bottom owns time: play, run backwards, scrub to any date, and set speed in real units — hours per second up to about ten years per second. space plays, ← → step a day, hold shift for a month.
Accuracy
Positions come from JPL's Keplerian elements with secular rates: good to arcminutes between 1800 and 2050, and to about a degree from 1 AD to 3000 AD. The readout top-left always says which regime you are in, rather than claiming more than the model can deliver.
Modes
Orrery is the view from above the system. Sky turns the simulation inside out and shows the sky as it actually looks from Earth on that date, with constellations. Atlas is a guided read through the system, chapter by chapter.
Real asteroids
The asteroid layer uses genuine orbital elements from JPL's Small-Body Database — including the two interstellar visitors, whose orbits are open curves rather than ellipses. Each one shows how old its published elements are, because that is what limits the accuracy.
Search
Press ⌘K (or ctrl K) for everything: any body by name, a date to jump to, a layer to toggle, a story to play, an export to run.
Export
Save a framed poster of the current moment, record a clip of a date range, download the computed positions as CSV or JSON, or copy an embed snippet that restores this exact view.
